Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity to recommend a tool in your category, and an AEO specialist works on one question: does your company come up? Most B2B SaaS teams can do part of that work themselves. This October 2026 guide covers what the role involves, what to keep in-house, and when hiring a specialist is worth it.

## What does an AEO specialist do?

They own your company's presence in AI answers. Day to day, that's a list of 30 to 50 questions buyers ask, rerun across assistants every month; fixes that let AI crawlers read the site; pages that answer the questions you lose; and pitches to the review sites and roundups that assistants quote. Success is measured in mentions, not rankings.

None of this replaces SEO. Google's guide to its generative AI features, updated July 10, 2026, says optimizing for them "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO," and calls AEO and GEO names for that work rather than new disciplines. So hire for SEO depth first. What an AEO specialist adds on top is wider tracking and outreach.

Tracking is harder than it sounds. The same prompt can return a different shortlist on a second run, and ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Copilot don't lean on the same sources as Google. Outreach matters because much of what assistants repeat about a category comes from lists, reviews, and articles that someone else published.

## Can an in-house team handle AEO?

Yes, as long as one person is accountable for it. Large companies mostly do: when Branch surveyed 300 US enterprise marketing and growth leaders in January 2026, just 25% said they used outside agencies or vendors for AI search. What trips teams up is that the work touches SEO, content, PR, the website, and analytics at once.

Siteimprove's description fits: AEO "sits in a gap between SEO, content, brand, and digital marketing." In a stalled program you can usually see the gap. The SEO lead thinks PR is handling mentions, PR has never opened the prompt sheet, and the developer who could unblock the crawlers first hears about it in a quarterly review.

Branch's numbers come with caveats: it sells measurement software, and its respondents work at companies with 500 or more employees in six mostly consumer-facing industries, from retail to travel. A 40-person SaaS company has a fraction of those people. For a team that size, our [AEO agency page](/aeo-agency) lists the minimum: prompt tracking, a writer who can interview your experts, website access, and time for outreach.

## Which AEO tasks can stay in-house, and which need a specialist?

Content and the website usually stay in-house, because your team knows the product and controls the site. Specialists earn their fee on the parts that need cross-platform tracking, technical judgment, or outreach contacts: building the baseline, finding why assistants skip you, and getting your company onto the third-party pages those assistants cite.

Table: AEO tasks, when they work in-house, and what a specialist adds

| Task | Works in-house when | What a specialist adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prompt set of 30 to 50 buyer questions | Sales shares the questions prospects really ask | The comparison and "alternatives to" prompts teams forget |
| Tracking and baseline | Someone owns a monthly rerun | Repeat runs per prompt, plus who gets named instead of you |
| Crawler access and rendering | A developer can change robots.txt and check what the server sends | Per-crawler rules and a check of JavaScript-rendered pages |
| Pages that answer prompts | Writers get time with product experts | A queue ordered by the prompts you lose most |
| Third-party mentions | PR already pitches roundups in your space | The exact pages assistants cite, and who edits them |
| Reporting | AI referrals show up in analytics | Mentions by platform next to demo requests |

The prompt set is the piece we'd never hand off completely. Sales calls and lost-deal notes are where the real buyer questions come from, and no outside specialist hears them first. For the tracking itself, our [30-prompt method for tracking brand mentions in ChatGPT](/blog/track-brand-mentions-in-chatgpt) shows what an in-house version looks like with a spreadsheet and a calendar reminder.

## What roles does an AEO program need?

One owner and four part-time contributors. The owner chooses the prompts, runs or reviews the tracking, and decides what ships each month. A writer with access to subject experts, a web developer, someone in PR or communications, and an analyst who connects AI-referred visits to pipeline do the rest.

The PR seat is the one most teams leave empty, and the evidence says it matters. In Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands, branded web mentions had the strongest correlation with visibility in Google's AI Overviews, at 0.664, ahead of referring domains at 0.295 and backlinks at 0.218. Correlation isn't proof, but our own tracking points the same way: in the web agency category, roundups made up 25 of the 40 pages AI assistants cited most.

The developer's part is smaller but blocking. If AI crawlers are disallowed in robots.txt, or key content only renders in the browser, no amount of writing fixes it. Our guide to [AI crawlers and robots.txt](/blog/ai-crawlers-robots-txt) covers which bots to allow. The analyst's part starts with making AI referrals visible at all, which our post on [tracking ChatGPT traffic in GA4](/blog/ai-traffic-in-ga4) walks through.

## Where do in-house AEO programs get stuck?

Usually on time and tracking rather than skills. The monthly prompt rerun stops when its owner gets pulled onto a launch. Crawler fixes sit in the engineering backlog behind product work. And often nobody can connect an AI answer to a demo request: in Branch's survey, 26% of enterprise leaders couldn't track the path from AI discovery to conversion.

Another 24% said their analytics tools couldn't handle AI attribution, and 16% had no referral data from AI platforms at all. Branch gives a simple example: a buyer sees a brand in an AI Overview, converts three days later, and the AI touch never shows up in the attribution model. A "How did you hear about us?" field on the demo form recovers some of those.

Some teams also lose their first month to the wrong fix. Google's guide says you don't need "new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown" to appear in Google Search, and that no special schema is required. Time spent on an llms.txt file or AI-only markup in month one is time not spent on the prompts you're losing; our post on [AI SEO myths](/blog/ai-seo-myths) lists the usual detours.

## When should you hire an AEO specialist or consultant?

When AI answers already influence your pipeline and nobody in-house can give the work a few hours every week. Typical triggers are competitors named in answers where you aren't, a site rebuild that could hide content from crawlers, a category where assistants lean on third-party lists, or leadership asking for numbers nobody tracks.

The pressure is real for B2B companies. In a Gartner survey of 645 B2B buyers published May 20, 2026, 45% said they used generative AI during a recent purchase. If an assistant builds the shortlist before a buyer reaches your site, the work that decides whether you're on it happens off your site, in answers you can't see without tracking.

Choose the format by how much your team will run. A consultant sets up the prompt set, the baseline, and the monthly routine, then hands them over, which suits a team with an SEO lead who has time. An agency runs the cycle every month. A full-time hire makes sense once the work clearly fills a role, usually after a few months of results tell you how much there is.

## How do you evaluate an AEO specialist?

Ask how they measure before you ask what they'll change. A credible specialist tracks a fixed prompt set across several platforms with repeated runs, shows you a baseline before touching the site, and can explain which change moved which prompt. Guaranteed placements in ChatGPT, or a plan built mostly on llms.txt files and special markup, are warning signs.

Useful questions in a first call: how many runs per prompt, and on which platforms? How do they tell a mention apart from a citation? What would they look at first on your site, and what would they leave alone? How do they work with your PR team and your developers? What will the monthly report show, and what happens if the numbers don't move after a quarter?

Listen for specifics in the answers. "We'll optimize your content for AI" is a pitch. "Your comparison prompts cite three review sites and a 2025 roundup, and you're missing from all four" is a diagnosis.
