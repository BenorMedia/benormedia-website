Webflow maintenance services from BenorMedia give B2B marketing teams one monthly team for their Webflow site: page and content updates, new landing pages, bug fixes, technical SEO monitoring, and quarterly conversion audits. Webflow already runs the hosting and the security patches, so our time goes into the site itself as your marketing changes.

## What Webflow handles, and what still needs a team.

A Webflow site doesn't need the plugin updates, server patches, and backup jobs that a WordPress site does, because Webflow runs that layer for every site it hosts. What Webflow doesn't do is change your site. New pages, CMS structure, broken forms, SEO issues, and conversion work still need someone who owns them every month.

According to [Webflow's hosting page](https://webflow.com/hosting), hosted sites run on AWS infrastructure with Cloudflare's CDN, get SSL certificates that renew automatically, and have automatic backups you can preview and restore. There are no updates to run or packages to install. The 99.99% uptime SLA comes only with Enterprise plans.

Table: Who looks after what on a Webflow site

| Task | Who handles it |
| --- | --- |
| Hosting, CDN, and scaling | Webflow, on AWS and Cloudflare |
| SSL certificates | Webflow, renewed automatically |
| Platform security updates | Webflow, with no plugins or packages to update |
| Backups | Webflow, automatic, plus manual save points |
| Uptime SLA | Webflow, 99.99% on Enterprise plans only |
| New pages, landing pages, and content updates | Your team or a maintenance partner |
| CMS structure and cleanup | Your team or a maintenance partner |
| Forms, CRM connections, analytics, and scripts | Your team or a maintenance partner |
| Technical SEO, redirects, and page speed | Your team or a maintenance partner |
| Conversion and UX improvements | Your team or a maintenance partner |

The bottom half of that table is the work of a Webflow maintenance service. It's also where sites drift after launch when nobody owns the work: requests pile up, a form stops sending leads to the CRM, or a URL change goes live without a redirect.

One team handles design, development, SEO, and conversion work for your Webflow site, so requests don't wait for a hand-off between vendors.

## How monthly support works.

You send requests, we plan them with you, and finished work ships every week. One project manager is your single point of contact, and the same designers and developers stay on your site, so nobody has to relearn your brand or your build each month.

- **One place for requests.** You add requests to a shared Trello board, where you can see what's in progress, what's next, and what's done.
- **A weekly rhythm.** We agree priorities with you, ship through the week, and tell you what went live.
- **A quarterly review.** We look at conversion, speed, and search data together and agree on the next quarter's improvements.
- **Your channel.** We work in your Slack or by email, whichever your team already uses.
- **Hours that overlap with yours.** We're based in Barcelona and work remotely. Our hours cover the European working day and overlap with every US time zone.

## Retainer, hours, or unlimited requests.

Webflow support is usually sold in three ways: a block of hours, an "unlimited requests" plan, or a monthly retainer. We work on monthly retainers, because a team that knows your site can plan around your marketing calendar instead of clearing a queue one ticket at a time.

Table: Three common Webflow support models

| Model | How it works | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block of hours | You buy hours up front and spend them on tasks as they come up | Sites with occasional, predictable changes |
| Unlimited requests | A flat monthly fee for a queue of tasks, worked in order | Small, frequent tasks with flexible deadlines |
| Monthly retainer | A set team and set capacity each month, planned against your roadmap | Sites that change every month and support pipeline goals |

## When you don't need a maintenance plan.

Some sites don't need a monthly team, and we'd rather say so up front.

- **Your site rarely changes.** If your team edits CMS content itself and the pages stay the same for months, Webflow's hosting covers the platform. Book a one-off project when something comes up.
- **You need a new site, not more updates.** If the site no longer fits your positioning, a [website redesign](page:website-redesign) comes first, and support can follow.
- **You need one fix.** A single scoped project makes more sense than a retainer for a one-time problem.
