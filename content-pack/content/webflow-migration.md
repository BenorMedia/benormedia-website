Moving from WordPress to Webflow is a URL project first and a design project second. We map every old URL to its new address, move and check every page by hand, and test every redirect before the switch. Your marketing team ends up with a site it can update without booking a developer.

30+ WordPress to Webflow migrations in the last 6 years back that process.

## Start with a map of every URL

Before anyone builds, we map what your site has to do and what must not break, and for a migration that starts with the URLs.

- **Technical audit.** A review of the current site and the integrations it depends on.
- **Content inventory.** Every page and post listed, so you decide what moves, what merges, and what retires.
- **Redirect map.** Every old URL paired with its new address before the build starts.
- **Sitemap architecture.** A new structure planned around the pages that already earn traffic.
- **Timeline and sign-off.** Timeline planning and stakeholder alignment before the first page is built.

A typical migration takes 3 to 4 weeks for a site of about 20 pages, 6 to 8 weeks for a mid-size site with a blog, and 10 to 14 weeks for a large site.

The full method is in our [guide to migrating from WordPress to Webflow](page:wordpress-to-webflow-migration).

## Rebuild in Webflow with a component library

Pages come out of a component system, so your team can assemble new layouts from pieces that already match instead of booking a developer.

- Your design translated into reusable components, variables, and design tokens.
- Standardized patterns for the page types you publish most.
- CMS architecture for posts, resources, and customer stories.
- Responsive layouts, interactions, and integrations built to your designs.

## Every page moved by hand, every redirect tested

Most rebuilds lose rankings during the migration step, so it gets the closest supervision in our process.

- **Content migration.** Every page moved and checked by hand.
- **SEO verification.** The new pages checked against the old ones.
- **Redirect testing.** Every redirect tested before the switch.
- **Launch coordination.** We are in the room while the site goes live.
- **Post-launch monitoring.** The site is monitored after the switch.

## Plugins, forms, and integrations: what moves and what is replaced

A WordPress plugin cannot run in Webflow. What a plugin did moves to a built-in Webflow setting, an integration, or custom code, unless that job is retired.

- SEO fields, sitemaps, and redirects have native settings in Webflow.
- [Webflow forms](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961347548563) store submissions and connect to tools such as HubSpot, Zapier, and Make.
- CRM, analytics, and marketing automation are connected during the build, when it is cheap, and not after launch.

## SEO and AI search readiness are part of the build

We structure every site for both traditional search and AI-first discovery.

- **Technical SEO.** The technical basics search engines need to crawl and index the new site.
- **Schema.** Structured data that tells search engines and AI assistants what each page is.
- **Semantic structure.** Headings and page structure that people and machines can both follow.
- **Answer engine optimization (AEO).** Content that AI assistants can understand, cite, and recommend.

Your marketers get live training, video walkthroughs, and documentation they will open, so running the site feels unremarkable.

- Live training and video tutorials.

- [Ongoing website support](/ongoing-website-support) when you want a team behind the site: weekly updates, technical SEO monitoring, and bug fixes.

A standard migration is a like-for-like move of your pages, CMS content, and redirects, and a redesign is scoped as its own project. We clean up the content as it moves, and support continues for 30 days after launch.

## When staying on WordPress is the better call

Not every WordPress site should move. Look hard at these cases before you commit:

- **A very large content library.** Compare your item counts with Webflow's current CMS limits first.
- **An online store on WooCommerce.** Confirm that Webflow's e-commerce features fit your catalog, checkout, and tax rules.
- **Member-only content.** Work out how logins and gated pages would work after the move.
- **Plugins that carry custom logic.** Decide what replaces each one before you commit.
- **Complex multi-language setups.** Confirm how language versions, URLs, and hreflang tags carry over.

If the site already works for your team and none of these cases fits, staying on WordPress is a fair answer. [Webflow vs WordPress](page:webflow-vs-wordpress) compares the two platforms.
